# Pilot and adoption plan

## Positioning to test

“When your community program moves or cancels, approve the correction once. Update connected notices and see what still needs attention.”

First audience: a community-center coordinator responsible for one recurring class. Beneficiary: residents who need current dates, hours, and venue information without another account.

## Discovery before expansion

Observe three coordinators reconstructing their most recent real schedule change. Ask where the information appeared, who approved the facts, how long each update took, which copies remained stale, and how they learned of errors. Do not imply partnerships or report synthetic activity as resident impact.

## Small pilot

Begin with one consenting organization, one program, and its owned page. Identify the coordinator authorized to confirm facts. Inventory the publication surfaces before collecting source material. Use a paired manual checklist to compare similar changes; count staff clarification and correction time as work, not just button-click time.

Record:

- Coordinator minutes per change, including review and exception handling.
- Time between confirmation and each destination's correction.
- Number of unresolved copies and how long they remain unresolved.
- Incorrect publications and human corrections needed.
- Whether the coordinator chooses to use the tool again for the next change.

Proposed continuation threshold: at least 30% less median coordinator time with no increase in incorrect publications, plus willingness to continue. This threshold is a decision rule, not an expected or measured outcome. Pause the pilot after any material incorrect publication and investigate the authority/verification boundary.

## Sharing mechanism already implemented

The coordinator can copy the permanent public notice link, download a QR code, and include it in generated flyers. Residents can open it without signing up. Partner editors can receive a copyable correction request; the prototype sends nothing automatically.

The proposed adoption path is: useful notice → partner organization sees it → partner asks to use the workflow for its own program. Measure referred organizations that actually complete a second update. Page views alone do not demonstrate community benefit or viral growth.

## Sustainable community use

Keep resident access free. Test hosted support funded by community networks, libraries, municipalities, or umbrella nonprofits. Explore contributing the temporary-change workflow to the Open Referral ecosystem if integration yields more value than a separate product. Real multi-organization onboarding and pricing are deferred until demand and operating cost are measured.

## Do not launch with these claims

No “all copies found,” “real-time everywhere,” “prevented wasted trips,” “partnered with 211,” live Spanish support, or confirmed directory integration without direct evidence. Rename the product before investing in acquisition because the ServiceSignal name already has unrelated uses.
